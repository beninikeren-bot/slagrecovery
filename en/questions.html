Industry questions

What actually happens to metal in slag, which methods work on which material, and where the assessment of a dump begins.

Why metal stays in the slagMetal enters slag not only as oxides. A significant share is mechanical inclusions and prills, established only by testing a sample. Total element content versus metallic phaseA standard plant assay does not separate the forms in which an element occurs. Recovery is calculated from the metallic phase, not total content. When magnetic separation works and when it does notMagnetic separation is not universal. Ferrosilicon prills are non-magnetic, manganese alloy separates poorly, and a magnet does not take sulphide copper. Strategies for the fine fractionThe fine fraction often carries metal but needs dedicated methods. Magnetic separation of fine prills is frequently a dead end. Processing wet sludgeSludge must be dewatered before it returns to production. Drying beds risk groundwater contamination; zinc in steelmaking sludge must be controlled. Assessing a dump: the tests you needDump assessment combines physical and chemical methods. Chemistry alone is not enough, because a dump is not homogeneous. The limits of a plant laboratoryA plant laboratory is built for melt control. Recovery projects need mineral processing instrumentation it does not usually have. Rules for taking a representative sampleA sampling error is not corrected by laboratory quality. Sample several zones, below the weathered layer, and keep a sealed reference portion.

R. Keren works with all secondary metallurgical materials. The processing route is determined by the properties of the specific material and confirmed in the laboratory during project work — a working step, not a restriction on the range of materials.
